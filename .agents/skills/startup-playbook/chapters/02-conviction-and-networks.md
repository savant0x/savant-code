# Evidence-Updated Conviction and Compounding Relationships

## Learning objectives

- Use evidence updates to distinguish contrarian conviction from stubbornness.
- Recognize the different warnings sent by "nobody believes this" and "everybody agrees."
- Understand networks as long-term relationship compounding rather than immediate transactions.

## Core ideas

Altman recommends choosing a direction that has only recently become possible, could become large, and is not yet believed by the mainstream—and preparing to be misunderstood for a long time. He also gives a guardrail: conviction should strengthen or weaken as more data arrives. The source defines no threshold for "enough data," so any concrete threshold must be labeled as an inference when this principle is applied. `claim-ch2-conviction`

For teams and networks, he offers a two-sided diagnostic. Finding nobody who shares the belief may indicate a problem with the hypothesis; immediate agreement from everyone may indicate a crowded direction. A more robust approach is to find a small number of genuine believers, enter high-quality networks, and offer many people small but real help without knowing whether it will ever be repaid. `claim-ch2-network`

## Demonstration from the video

The OpenAI story at 12:43–14:43 illustrates a contrarian direction, prolonged misunderstanding, and conviction that continued to build from new data. ## Evidence modality

This chapter does not use visuals to verify team networks or historical causality. These accounts remain retrospective claims by the speaker and are supported only by timestamped speech.

## Common failure modes

- Treating opposition itself as proof of being right.
- Collecting only supporting material without defining what evidence would weaken the belief.
- Turning networking into a transaction in which every return is calculated.
- Treating location advice as permanent; the source explicitly acknowledges that its Bay Area advice may become outdated.

## Knowledge check

Why can both "nobody agrees with me" and "everybody agrees with me" be warning signs? Your answer must state what data should be collected next.

## Micro-exercise

Create three columns for your non-consensus hypothesis: observed evidence, new evidence that would strengthen it, and new evidence that would weaken it. Then name one person you could help in a small way without making that help conditional on a return.
