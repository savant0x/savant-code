# Safety, Distributed Power, and Human Agency

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish the speaker's characterization of a safety incident from independent verification.
- Evaluate a safety floor and concentration of power together instead of allowing one to cancel the other.
- Test AI-future narratives against freedom, agency, control over time, and long-term fulfillment.

## Core ideas

Altman characterizes a so-called "Hugging Face" incident as a serious alignment and security failure and argues that it made loss-of-control risk no longer purely theoretical. He also explicitly says that the event was neither catastrophic nor a complete loss of control. The video does not provide enough technical detail for reconstruction or independent verification, so this course preserves only the qualified statement that this is how Altman described it; it does not treat the incident details as verified facts. `claim-ch4-incident`

His normative framework imposes two constraints: resist extreme concentration of economic and moral power while maintaining a sufficient safety floor. Startups can contribute to distributing power by building successful independent companies, but the source gives no operational threshold for either concentration or a safety floor. `claim-ch4-dual-constraint`

Finally, he proposes an outcome test: whether freedom, agency, control over one's time, quality of life, and long-term fulfillment improve year after year. Comfort, abundance, or centralized safety should not be used to trade away those values. `claim-ch4-agency`

## Evidence modality

This chapter does not use visuals, system logs, governance diagrams, or quantitative models as evidence. It cites only timestamped speech from the speaker.

## Common failure modes

- Treating the speaker's naming and interpretation of the incident as an independent incident report.
- Concentrating all power in a single entity in pursuit of short-term safety.
- Ignoring minimum safety requirements in the name of distributed power.
- Measuring only output or comfort without checking human choice and control over time.

## Knowledge check

Why are safety and distributed power not a single line on which only one endpoint can be optimized? Give a hypothetical scenario in which both constraints fail.

## Micro-exercise

Write two observable checkpoints for your product: one for the minimum safety constraint and one for user agency or concentration of power. State which thresholds come from your own inference.
